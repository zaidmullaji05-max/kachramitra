# E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022

These rules replaced the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2016 and took effect on 1 April 2023.
They are built on **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**: whoever puts electrical and
electronic equipment on the market must finance its collection and environmentally sound
recycling.

## What counts as e-waste

Anything with a plug, battery, or circuit board at the end of its life: mobile phones,
laptops, chargers and cables, batteries, CFLs and tube lights, TVs and monitors, printers,
routers, small appliances, and the electronic parts inside them. The rules cover a long
list of equipment categories rather than a single definition.

## The one rule that matters most

E-waste must go to a **registered producer take-back system, a collection centre, or an
authorised dismantler/recycler**. It must not be:

- given to an informal scrap dealer who breaks, burns, or acid-leaches it,
- mixed with household dry waste,
- stored indefinitely at home "for later".

Informal recycling recovers copper and gold with acid baths and open burning, releasing
lead, mercury, and dioxins, and it is exactly what the rules exist to stop.

## Producer and bulk-consumer duties

- Producers must set up take-back and collection, meet annual collection targets, and
  display collection points.
- Bulk consumers (offices, schools, hotels, hospitals) must hand e-waste only to authorised
  channels and keep records.
- Refurbishers and dismantlers must be registered and follow storage and processing norms.

## Practical guidance for households

1. Wipe your data and remove SIM/memory cards before handing over a phone or laptop.
2. Keep the charger with the device; recyclers recover more material from a complete unit.
3. Ask for a receipt or acknowledgement when you hand over items.
4. Brand service centres usually accept their own products; municipal e-waste bins and
   CPCB-listed recyclers accept the rest.
5. Never put a battery, CFL, or tube light in the regular bin — see the battery and mercury
   sections.

## Verification

CPCB publishes the list of registered recyclers and the EPR portal; state pollution control
boards publish local collection points. Tell users to verify with the municipal corporation
or the brand's take-back page for their city.
