# Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016

Summary for practical use. Always verify current local practice with your municipal
corporation; states add their own bye-laws.

## Segregation at source

The rules require waste to be segregated at the source into three streams:

- **Wet / biodegradable waste** (green bin): kitchen peelings, cooked food, garden waste.
- **Dry / recyclable waste** (blue bin): paper, cardboard, plastic, glass, metal, tetra-paks, cloth.
- **Domestic hazardous waste** (red or black bin, collected separately): paints, pesticides,
  aerosols, mercury-containing items, waste oil, and similar.

Mixing the streams defeats the purpose: one wet bag can spoil an entire load of dry
recyclables, and hazardous waste in a dry-waste truck exposes collection workers to injury
and poisoning.

## Who must compost

Households and housing societies that generate a large quantity of wet waste (bulk waste
generators, typically 100 kg/day or more, and gated communities as notified by the local
body) must compost or process wet waste on-site instead of handing it to the municipality.

Even below that threshold, home composting or a society composter cuts the waste you send
to landfill by roughly half by weight.

## Duties of waste generators

- Segregate as above and hand waste only to the authorised collector.
- Do not burn waste in the open; open burning of plastic and mixed waste is prohibited.
- Do not litter or throw waste into drains, water bodies, or on vacant land.
- Pay the user fee notified by the local body where applicable.
- Keep sanitary waste wrapped separately and marked, so collectors can handle it safely.

## Collection and processing

Local bodies must arrange door-to-door collection, transport segregated streams separately,
and process wet waste locally (composting, bio-methanation). Dry waste goes to material
recovery facilities; rejects go to sanitary landfill or waste-to-energy. Landfilling of
segregated wet waste and recyclables is not permitted.

## What this means for the app

When the user asks "which bin", answer with the three-stream model and name the local
practice as a caveat. Never tell a user to burn waste or dump it in a drain.
