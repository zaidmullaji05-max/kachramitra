# Practical household guide

The rules are the law; this page is what actually works in an Indian kitchen.

## The three-bin setup

- **Green** — wet, biodegradable: peels, cooked food, tea leaves, garden clippings, eggshells.
- **Blue** — dry, recyclable: paper, cardboard, clean plastic, glass, metal, tetra-paks, cloth.
- **Red/black** — domestic hazardous: batteries, CFLs, paints, pesticides, aerosols, waste oil,
  expired medicines, sharps (in a rigid sealed container).

A fourth, optional bin: **sanitary waste**, wrapped and marked, handed over separately.

## The two rules that fix most mistakes

1. **If it has food on it, it is wet waste until it is clean.** A greasy pizza box, a ketchup
   sachet, or a half-full curd cup contaminates a whole bale of recyclables.
2. **Nothing hazardous goes in the green or blue bin.** One battery in a dry-waste truck is a
   fire; one CFL is mercury vapour on the sorting line.

## Home composting in a flat

- A 20–30 litre bucket or a terracotta pot with a lid and drainage holes is enough.
- Layer wet waste with dry leaves, shredded paper, or sawdust (roughly equal volumes) to stop
  smell and flies.
- Turn it every few days; keep it as damp as a wrung-out sponge.
- It is ready in 6–10 weeks, when it is dark, crumbly, and smells like soil.
- Do not add meat, fish, dairy in quantity, oily food, or pet waste to a small bin.

## Common items, quick answers

| Item | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Plastic bottle | rinse, flatten, blue bin |
| Milk pouch | rinse, dry, blue bin (MLP — check local acceptance) |
| Chips packet / sachet | rinse, dry, dry waste; usually not recyclable |
| Tetra-pak | rinse, flatten, blue bin |
| Glass bottle | blue bin, wrap if broken |
| Broken cup / ceramic | general waste (not recyclable glass) |
| Old clothes | donation first; otherwise dry waste |
| Battery | taped terminals, e-waste collection point |
| CFL / tube light | e-waste point, mercury warning |
| Old phone / charger | e-waste take-back or authorised recycler |
| Syringe / needle | rigid sealed container, biomedical point |
| Paint / pesticide | sealed original container, hazardous collection |
| Expired medicine | pharmacy take-back, not the drain |
| Sanitary waste | wrapped, marked, separate handover |
| Soiled plastic / food wrapper | general/mixed waste if it cannot be cleaned |

## What never to do

- Do not burn waste, in a bin or in the open.
- Do not dump in a drain, river, vacant plot, or the sea.
- Do not give e-waste to an informal dealer who breaks or burns it.
- Do not mix sharps with household waste.
- Do not put wet waste into the recyclable stream "because it will be sorted anyway".

## When the answer is local

Which bin colour your municipal body uses, whether it collects MLP, and which shop takes
batteries all vary by city. The honest answer is the general rule plus "check with your
municipal corporation". Guessing a colour scheme is worse than saying it varies.
