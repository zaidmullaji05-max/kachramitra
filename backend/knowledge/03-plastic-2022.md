# Plastic Waste Management Rules

India's plastic rules have been amended several times. The parts a household actually needs
are the single-use plastic ban, the thickness rule for carry bags, and the EPR-driven
recycling obligations on producers.

## The single-use plastic ban

Since 1 July 2022 the manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale and use of identified
single-use plastic items is prohibited. The list includes:

- earbuds with plastic sticks, plastic sticks for balloons and flags
- plastic candy and ice-cream sticks
- cutlery (forks, knives, spoons, straws, stirrers, plates, cups, glasses)
- plastic carry bags thinner than the permitted thickness
- PVC banners under 100 microns
- sweet-box wrapping film and cigarette-pack wrapping film
- expanded polystyrene (thermocol) for decoration and cups/plates

Items that are merely *packaging* are handled through EPR rather than the ban list, so a
multi-layer chips packet is legal but is **not** recyclable in the ordinary sense.

## Thickness and carry bags

Plastic carry bags and plastic sheets must meet a minimum thickness (raised over time to 120
microns for carry bags). Thin bags tear into fragments that cannot be recovered, which is why
the rule exists.

## Extended Producer Responsibility

Producers, importers and brand owners of plastic packaging must register on the CPCB EPR
portal, meet annual recycling and reuse targets, and pay for collection. This is the reason
brand take-back schemes and store drop-offs exist — use them where available.

## What is actually recyclable

| Plastic | Typical use | Household handling |
|---|---|---|
| PET (1) | water and soft-drink bottles | rinse, flatten, blue dry-waste bin or kabadiwala |
| HDPE (2) | shampoo, detergent, milk pouches (rigid) | rinse, dry, blue bin |
| LDPE (4) | carry bags, bubble wrap, thin film | only clean and dry; often not accepted |
| PP (5) | food containers, caps, buckets | rinse, blue bin |
| PS (6) | disposable cups, thermocol | avoid; mostly not recyclable locally |
| PVC (3) | pipes, blister packs | not recyclable at household level |
| multi-layer | chips, biscuit, shampoo sachets | not recyclable at household level; dry waste or refuse-derived fuel |

## Multi-layer packaging (MLP)

A sachet or chips packet is several materials fused together — plastic film, aluminium foil,
adhesive. Separating them is rarely economic, so most Indian MRFs will not take them. It is
honest to tell the user: rinse and dry it, put it in dry waste if your collection accepts MLP,
otherwise it goes to the general/mixed stream. Do **not** claim it is recyclable.

## Practical guidance

- The number in the triangle is a resin code, not a promise that your city recycles it.
- Rinse and dry anything with food residue; one wet container spoils a bale.
- Keep caps on (they are usually PP and MRFs accept them with the bottle).
- Flatten bottles to cut transport volume.
- Never burn plastic: burning PVC and MLP releases dioxins and hydrochloric acid.

## Verification

Local bodies publish which plastic grades their MRF accepts. If the answer depends on the
city, say so and point to the municipal corporation rather than guessing.
