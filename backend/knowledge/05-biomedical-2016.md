# Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016

These rules govern waste generated by hospitals, clinics, laboratories, blood banks,
veterinary facilities, and research institutions. Household biomedical waste is a grey area:
the rules bind healthcare facilities, while municipal solid-waste rules tell households how
to hand over sanitary waste and sharps.

## The colour-coded streams (for facilities)

- **Yellow** — human/animal anatomical waste, soiled waste, expired medicines, chemical waste.
- **Red** — contaminated recyclable plastic (tubing, bottles, gloves).
- **White (translucent)** — sharps: needles, syringes, blades, broken glass.
- **Blue** — glassware, metallic implants, other recyclable contaminated items.

Facilities must hand each stream to a Common Bio-medical Waste Treatment Facility (CBWTF)
and keep manifests.

## Household sharps

Used insulin needles, lancets, and injection needles must **never** go loose into a bin or a
plastic bag — they injure collection workers and can transmit blood-borne infection.

1. Do not recap, bend, or break a needle.
2. Put it in a puncture-proof container: a rigid plastic or metal box with a lid (a detergent
   or sharps container). Do not use a thin bottle.
3. Seal it when it is about three-quarters full and label it "sharps".
4. Hand it to a hospital/clinic that accepts household sharps, a pharmacy take-back point, or
   a municipal biomedical waste collection point — call ahead and confirm.

## Household sanitary waste

Sanitary napkins, diapers, and tampons: wrap them in a newspaper or a paper bag, mark them
so collectors know, and hand them over separately as instructed by your local body. Do not
flush them, and do not put loose sharps or sanitary waste in the wet or dry recyclable stream.

## Expired medicines

Do not flush medicines down the drain and do not put them loose in wet waste. Take them to a
pharmacy take-back point or a municipal collection drive. Keep them in the original blister or
bottle so they are identifiable.

## Masks, gloves, and rapid test kits

A used mask or glove from a healthy household goes in general waste (tied shut). A test kit
and anything blood-stained goes in a sealed bag, then to the biomedical collection point.

## Verification

Which collection point accepts household sharps is local. Tell the user to call the municipal
corporation health department or the nearest hospital, and never claim a specific counter
accepts them without confirming.
