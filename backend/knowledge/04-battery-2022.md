# Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022

These rules replaced the Batteries (Management and Handling) Rules, 2001. They cover every
battery chemistry — lead-acid, lithium-ion, nickel-cadmium, zinc-carbon, alkaline, button
cells — and are built on Extended Producer Responsibility.

## Why batteries are not ordinary waste

- **Lithium-ion** cells can go into thermal runaway: a punctured or swollen cell can ignite
  and is very hard to extinguish. Fires in garbage trucks and landfill sites are commonly
  traced to batteries.
- **Lead-acid** batteries contain lead and sulphuric acid.
- **Nickel-cadmium** contains cadmium, a carcinogen.
- **Button cells** often contain mercury or silver and are small enough to be swallowed.

The rules require collection and recycling through registered channels precisely because a
battery in a mixed-waste truck is a fire and a poisoning risk.

## What the rules require

- Producers must collect waste batteries back and meet collection targets.
- Collection, segregation, transport, refurbishing and recycling must be done by registered
  entities, with records.
- Informal dismantling, open burning, and acid leaching of battery scrap are prohibited.
- Import of waste batteries is restricted.

## Household handling

1. **Tape the terminals.** Both terminals of a lithium or lead battery, with insulating tape.
   This prevents a short circuit against other metal in the bag.
2. **Do not puncture, crush, bend or open** a cell. Do not put it in water.
3. **Do not charge a swollen, leaking, or hot battery.** Move it somewhere non-flammable
   outdoors, away from children and pets, and take it to a collection point.
4. Hand it to a brand take-back counter, an authorised e-waste recycler, or a municipal
   e-waste collection point. Many electronics stores accept used cells.
5. Keep a leaking cell in a sealed plastic bag inside a rigid container.

## What if a battery is already swollen or burning

- Swollen or leaking: isolate outdoors, do not use it, do not charge it, transport it in a
  rigid non-flammable container, and contact the brand or the collection point before moving it.
- Burning or smoking: get away, call the fire service (101) or emergency (112). Do not try to
  smother a lithium fire with water in a confined space; a Class D extinguisher is the right tool.
  This is not a do-it-yourself situation.

## Verification

CPCB and state pollution control boards publish registered battery recyclers and collection
points. Brand take-back pages list store counters. Always say "verify with the collection
point" for a specific address.
